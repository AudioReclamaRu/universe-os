# UNIVERSE / GIZA PRIME / RSE
# Canonical Mathematical Pattern Atlas
## The common reference base for every search, comparison and reconstruction

## Canonical status and purpose

**This atlas is the common reference system for all research in UNIVERSE 2.0, UNIVERSE OS, GIZA PRIME, RSE, the Great Comma and connected projects.**

It is not a decorative catalogue, an appendix, a list of interesting numbers or a closed encyclopedia. It is the persistent working base against which every new observation, equation, geometry, spectrum, physical process, archaeological relation, geological structure, dynamic regime or numerical coincidence must be compared.

The research domain includes:
- every spatial extent, separation, shape, wavelength, resolution and geometric relation;
- every duration, period, delay, lifetime, memory time and temporal relation;
- every physical carrier, field, material, structure, environment and process;
- every mathematical object, transformation, invariant, recurrence, operator and pattern that may describe them.

No scale, discipline, phenomenon or mathematical family is privileged in advance. The organizing object is the **relation network**.

The atlas is intentionally open-ended. Mathematics contains infinitely many definable structures, and empirical discovery is unbounded. The goal is a comprehensive, structured and extensible search base—not a false claim that a finite document can enumerate everything.

## Governing rules

1. **Every new relation is compared against the entire atlas**, not only the section where it first appears.
2. Search for exact identities, approximate correspondences, structural isomorphisms, recurring operators, common exponents, invariant ratios, shared spectra, symmetry correspondences and dynamic analogies.
3. Compare direct values and relations between values: ratios, inverse ratios, differences, sums, products, powers, roots, cross-ratios, continued fractions, recurrence coefficients, spectral ratios, phase relations, scaling exponents and transfer functions.
4. Compare structures, not only numbers: graph topology, symmetry groups, root systems, face lattices, fractal construction rules, automata, differential equations, eigenoperators, attractors, boundary conditions and conservation laws.
5. Keep every interesting correspondence in the register, including weak and unresolved ones. A coincidence is a research lead, not a result to erase.
6. Distinguish exact mathematical identities from approximate numerical matches, empirical regularities, physical mechanisms and historical interpretations.
7. Do not turn a resemblance into causation without a physical path, dimensional consistency, source/receiver definition, energy accounting, uncertainty analysis and discriminating test.
8. A missing measurement is not zero. Build explicit idealized candidates and preserve alternatives; label them as idealizations rather than observations.
9. Use null models and multiple-comparison controls, but do not use them to suppress candidate discoveries. They determine how strong a correspondence is and what must be tested next.
10. When the next research step is unclear, return to the canonical motto: **«Отсутствие доказательства не является доказательством отсутствия»**. Expand the search space, inspect missing mechanisms and construct the broadest defensible candidate set.

## Numeral convention — paired binary and ternary

Integer keys and integer coefficients are written as a paired numeral:

\[
\langle n_2\mid n_3\rangle,
\]

where the left component is the base-two representation and the right component is the base-three representation of the same integer.

The basic keys are:

\[
\mathbf0=\langle0_2\mid0_3\rangle,\qquad
\mathbf1=\langle1_2\mid1_3\rangle,
\]

\[
\mathbf2=\langle10_2\mid2_3\rangle,\qquad
\mathbf3=\langle11_2\mid10_3\rangle,
\]

\[
\mathbf4=\langle100_2\mid11_3\rangle,\qquad
\mathbf5=\langle101_2\mid12_3\rangle,
\]

\[
\mathbf7=\langle111_2\mid21_3\rangle,\qquad
\mathbf8=\langle1000_2\mid22_3\rangle.
\]

A paired integer may be abbreviated by the bold symbol once defined. Exact irrational quantities remain symbolic: \(\pi,e,\varphi,\sqrt{\mathbf2},\sqrt{\mathbf3}\), etc. Their ordinary decimal expansions are not used as the project's primary representation.

A distinction is mandatory:

- positional base-two/base-three numerals are representations of the same integer;
- the binary state alphabet \(\{0,1\}\) is a model of distinguishable states;
- the ternary face-status alphabet \(\{-,0,+\}\) is a model of lower-fixed / free / upper-fixed coordinates;
- dynamic relation states may also use a ternary alphabet, but their meanings must be declared separately.

A shared alphabet does not imply identical mathematical operations or physical meanings. The ordinary decimal notation is not the project's preferred representation, but source identifiers, DOI strings, publication years and externally defined metadata remain verbatim where alteration would corrupt traceability.

## I. The fractal relation passport

For each self-similar construction, record:
- number of copies \(N\);
- contraction ratios \(r_i\);
- rotations, reflections and translations;
- overlap conditions;
- open-set or separation conditions;
- Hausdorff/box dimensions;
- spectral and walk dimensions, where defined;
- measure distribution;
- lacunarity and anisotropy;
- generator and substitution rule;
- discrete or continuous scale invariance;
- log-periodic corrections;
- robustness under perturbation.

For an equal-ratio self-similar construction satisfying the usual separation conditions, the similarity dimension solves:

\[
\sum_i r_i^D=\mathbf1.
\]

When all contraction factors are equal, this reduces to:

\[
D=\frac{\ln N}{\ln(1/r)}.
\]

This formula is not blindly applied to overlapping constructions, nonuniform measures, or fractals whose Hausdorff dimension is not determined by the similarity equation.

## AO. How to search for coincidences without losing discoveries

The project explicitly seeks coincidences and recurring ratios. Use a tiered classification:

- **Exact identity:** follows from definitions or a theorem.
- **Exact structural correspondence:** an isomorphism or mapping preserves specified relations.
- **Ideal target:** exact relation imposed in an explicitly idealized reconstruction.
- **Strong numerical candidate:** close under a justified uncertainty model and independently defined quantities.
- **Exploratory resemblance:** interesting match but incomplete data, uncertain mapping or post-selection.
- **Rejected under a declared test:** the match does not survive a specified null model, precision or alternative representation.
- **Unresolved:** insufficient information; retained as an open branch.

A numerical candidate is not deleted because it lacks a mechanism. It remains visible while the mechanism is sought. Its status must not be inflated either.

## AP. Null models and multiplicity

When comparing many constants and many observations, chance matches are inevitable. Therefore:
- retain the raw candidate and its discovery context;
- record how many candidate comparisons were made;
- compare with null values preserving range, units, dimension, sample size, distribution and geometric constraints;
- use alternative reference points and coordinate systems;
- test sensitivity to rounding and parameter uncertainty;
- reserve independent data for confirmation;
- record unsuccessful comparisons and negative results;
- avoid selecting a parameter after seeing the desired match without marking the analysis exploratory.

The null model is a test of strength, not a censor that removes a potentially important relation from the atlas.

## AQ. Relation quality ladder

Use two independent labels:

**Mathematical relation status**
- identity / theorem;
- exact isomorphism;
- exact embedding or projection;
- asymptotic equivalence;
- numerical approximation;
- structural analogy;
- conjecture.

**Empirical / physical status**
- directly measured;
- source-reported;
- indirectly inferred;
- reconstructed;
- idealized;
- physically modelled;
- untested candidate;
- contradicted under a declared test.

A mathematical identity can be exact while its proposed physical interpretation remains untested.
