# GIZA PRIME — Phase 05
# Purpose architecture: landscape datum, quarry geometry, parent masses and ideal pyramid network

## Canonical research question

**We do not reconstruct whether people measured, aimed, checked and adjusted the Giza landscape. We reconstruct WHY they did it and what whole-system result they were pursuing.**

The working picture is the maximum landscape system, not a minimum list of surviving monuments. Its nodes are the three pyramids, the Great Sphinx parent outcrop, the Khentkaus parent outcrop, the quarry landscape, temples, causeways, trenches, survey marks and the still-unlocated western alternative mass.

All numeric values in this phase use paired binary/ternary notation:

\[
\langle n_2\mid n_3\rangle
\]

The two numerals represent the same integer. Lengths, areas, volumes and ratios are written as fractions of paired integers, or symbolically where an exact irrational constant is involved. Source identifiers and bibliographic URLs are preserved unchanged.

## 1. Main system-level finding

The first coherent functional layer is a **landscape-scale geometric registration, quarry-control and construction-coordination architecture**.

The Giza plateau was not simply a blank surface on which three pyramids were independently placed. The natural bedrock relief, quarry boundaries, reserved rock masses, external datum lines, holes, corner trenches, causeway axes and temple limits formed a connected geometric working environment.

The evidence supports four tightly related operations:

1. **Natural datum selection:** retain and expose major bedrock masses and stratigraphic surfaces as persistent reference geometry.
2. **Survey registration:** establish external lines, squares, offsets, sight lines and corner controls where the pyramid base itself was not yet level or fully defined.
3. **Quarry and material control:** organise the extraction field, estimate available rock, track quarry limits and relate the removed volume to construction demand.
4. **Progressive construction refinement:** refine orientation, base position and level in successive passes as the bedrock and casing surfaces were prepared.

This is a first functional layer, not a reduction of the whole Giza system to quarry logistics. We continue to investigate the larger purpose and any additional mechanical, hydrological, acoustic, astronomical, electromagnetic, thermal or other system functions.

The bearings were reported as S36°14′W and S45°14′W respectively. ### Consequence for the ideal model

The two masses must be reconstructed together as geological reference objects:

- \(M_S\): reconstruct the parent outcrop before enclosure quarrying, head carving, weathering and repairs;
- \(M_{Kw}\): reconstruct the larger Khentkaus-area parent outcrop before quarrying, separation of the quarry cube, later tomb construction and surrounding reuse.

The stratigraphic correspondence gives us a way to correlate the two masses by geological layer and dip/strike, rather than by visual resemblance alone. It does not require the original masses to have had identical external shapes.

## 5. Khentkaus-centred quarry circle and the Khufu registration

Lehner's plateau interpretation identifies the Khentkaus monument as a corner-post-like feature in a horseshoe quarry within a larger circular depression. His approximate spatial construction is:

\[
R^*=\langle11001000_2\mid21102_3\rangle\ {\rm m}
\]

\[
D^*=\langle110010000_2\mid112211_3\rangle\ {\rm m}
\]

Thus the ideal circle has symbolic circumference and area:

\[
C^*=2\pi R^*,\qquad A^*=\pi(R^*)^2.
\]

The ideal circle is a mathematical registration layer. The excavated quarry is irregular and does not have to trace a perfect circle.

The observed relations used to build this ideal are:

- the Khafre causeway lies about one radius north of the Khentkaus monument;
- the western quarry edge lies about one radius west;
- the quarry boundary east of Khentkaus appears at a similar radial distance, with the surface partly concealed by sand;
- the horseshoe quarry widens northward to just over \(\langle111000001001110000_2\mid102200111112_3\rangle/\langle1_2\mid1_3\rangle\) m;
- the north width is close to the Khufu base side, \(\langle111000001110111001_2\mid102200221201_3\rangle/\langle1111101000_2\mid1101001_3\rangle\) m;
- the quarry's eastern and western boundaries align rather neatly with the eastern and western sides of Khufu;
- the Khafre causeway approaches the ideal circle tangentially and continues on a linear bedrock ramp;
- the Qibli knoll and the buried southern wadi provide additional candidate limits to the wider extraction field.

The north quarry width currently used as a map-derived estimate is about \(\langle11100110_2\mid22112_3\rangle\) m. Its ratio to the nominal Khufu base side is:

## 6. Material accounting: the “hole” and the “pile”

The Great Pyramid quarry survey gives these approximate gross volumes:

- removed quarry volume estimate: \(\langle1010100001110101000000_2\mid12012020000020_3\rangle\ {\rm m}^3\);
- nominal Khufu pyramid volume: \(\langle110001011100011001001001_2\mid220101111122010_3\rangle/\langle101_2\mid12_3\rangle\ {\rm m}^3\).

The present gross bounding-volume ratio is:

\[
\frac{V_{\rm quarry,gross}}{V_{\rm Khufu}}
=
\frac{\langle100000011111100_2\mid211211011_3\rangle}
{\langle11110100001001_2\mid210102201_3\rangle}.
\]

This is a near material-balance relation between a quarry depression and the pyramid volume. The quarry is neither a rectangular prism nor a uniform-depth pit; its floor rises, boundaries curve, the southern extent is buried, channels remove extra rock, and some blocks/waste remain. The ratio is therefore a first-order volume registration, not a final mass balance.

The ideal ledger is:

\[
V_{\rm gross\ extraction}
=
V_{\rm delivered\ blocks}
+
V_{\rm channel/wedge\ losses}
+
V_{\rm breakage\ and\ waste}
+
V_{\rm retained\ or\ inaccessible\ rock}.
\]

Then:

\[
V_{\rm delivered\ blocks}
=
V_{\rm pyramids}
+
V_{\rm temples}
+
V_{\rm causeways}
+
V_{\rm other\ plateau\ construction}
+
V_{\rm losses\ in\ transport/working}.
\]

The Khentkaus bedrock datum can therefore be tested as a quarry benchmark for monitoring the depth, area and progress of extraction. Lehner explicitly proposed volume calculation or monitoring work as a possible benchmark role.

For each orthogonal section with base span \(B\), height \(H\), half-base \(a=B/2\) and face apothem \(l\):

\[
l=\sqrt{H^2+a^2},\qquad
\eta=\frac{l}{a}=\sqrt{1+\left(\frac{2H}{B}\right)^2}.
\]

The current nominal dimensions yield this candidate pattern:
- Khufu: \(\eta\) is close to \(\varphi\);
- Khafre: \(H/B\) is close to the simple \(2:3\) relation and \(\eta\) is close to \(5:3\);
- Menkaure: the section using the shorter base span gives \(\eta\) extraordinarily close to \(\varphi\); the longer-span section does not.

The candidate sequence is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\varphi\ ;\ \frac53\ ;\ \varphi}
\]

for Khufu, Khafre and the selected Menkaure short-span section. This is a candidate ideal family to compare against alternative original heights, casing geometry and the orientation of Menkaure's rectangular base. It is not imposed on the longer Menkaure section.

## 9. Eight half-faces on every pyramid

Every pyramid is represented with four cardinal side sectors, each split into two ordered half-face patches:

\[
\mathcal H_i=
\{N_L,N_R,E_L,E_R,S_L,S_R,W_L,W_R\}.
\]

This is maintained for Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure. Each patch has its own profile \(\Delta_{i,s}(z)\), sagitta \(c_{i,s}(z)\), surface normal, curvature, course label and uncertainty/idealisation status.

### Empirical calibration and idealisation

Selected total-station half-wall fits at Menkaure provide the observed positive angular differences:

\[
\langle110010_2\mid1212_3\rangle',
\langle111101_2\mid2021_3\rangle',
\langle10101_2\mid210_3\rangle',
\langle100100_2\mid1100_3\rangle',
\langle100101_2\mid1101_3\rangle'.
\]

Their common mean ideal is:

\[
\Delta_{\rm H1}=\langle101001_2\mid1112_3\rangle'=41'.
\]

The median is:

\[
\Delta_{\rm med}=\langle100101_2\mid1101_3\rangle'=37'.
\]

The measured sensitivity range is \(21'\) to \(61'\). These are selected Menkaure courses, not direct measurements of all twelve pyramid sides. The ideal family intentionally transfers a common candidate parameter across all three pyramids so that the system can be compared as one designed arrangement.

For the symmetric V-profile ideal, if \(\Delta\) is the total difference between the fitted directions of the two half-faces, each half turns inward by \(\Delta/2\). The central inward sagitta is:

\[
\boxed{c(B,\Delta)=\frac{B}{2}\tan\left(\frac{\Delta}{2}\right)}
\]

with \(\Delta\) expressed as an angle.

## 11. The cumulative measuring sequence — why the traces matter

Lehner's layout study describes a successive-approximation process:
- bedrock core masses were first isolated and roughly dressed;
- lines of holes around Khufu provided possible cord/post positions, maintaining direction and reducing sag;
- the Khufu external hole line was about three metres from the foundation platform, with an average spacing of about seven cubits;
- Khafre had outer hole lines, staggered paired holes and corner trenches, with a separate external datum because the true base line was cut in the granite casing;
- further lines and offsets refined the layout square and the pyramid base;
- a related line of holes along the Khafre causeway may have defined its axis before construction;
- the holes, trenches and bedrock troughs could form a reference system for alignment, orientation and levelling while building proceeded.

The paper explicitly describes the method as successive approximation: the initial layout was refined in tandem with progressively finer dressing and levelling of the court. Some of the features could also have served later construction or drainage roles; the complete operational sequence is a reconstruction target.

This supplies a real engineering precedent for our system model: **the reference geometry was external, redundant and progressively refined.** The pyramids and natural masses were not isolated measurements; they were registered against a wider set of axes, offsets and physical datum marks.

## 12. System dynamics — a network of geometry, geology and material flow

Define the system state as:

\[
\mathcal G(t)=
\left[
\mathcal P(t),\mathcal M(t),\mathcal Q(t),
\mathcal A(t),\mathcal R(t),\mathcal F(t)
\right]
\]

where:
- \(\mathcal P\): pyramid geometry, eight half-faces and their local orientations;
- \(\mathcal M\): Sphinx and Khentkaus parent-mass envelopes and stratigraphic surfaces;
- \(\mathcal Q\): quarry boundary, volume, channels, ramps and extraction fronts;
- \(\mathcal A\): axes, baselines, datum squares, offsets, holes and corner controls;
- \(\mathcal R\): distance/angle/ratio invariants across all nodes;
- \(\mathcal F\): material, water, mechanical, acoustic, seismic, thermal and electromagnetic flows to be tested.

The design-registration problem is:

\[
\text{Find }\mathcal G^*
\text{ that jointly satisfies the geometric, geological, quarry and construction constraints.}
\]

The exact cross-pair equality is one constraint; the Khentkaus-centered quarry ideal, Khufu side registration, Khafre causeway tangent, stratigraphic tie and eight-half-face geometry are others.

Only after the geometry is registered should we populate a physical dynamic model such as:

\[
M\ddot q+C\dot q+Kq=F(t).
\]

The matrix blocks must derive from actual material properties, interfaces, geometry and measurements. Do not infer mechanical, acoustic, hydrological or electromagnetic coupling from distance alone.

## 13. Phase 04 corrections and supersession

This Phase 05 replaces or clarifies earlier Phase 04 assumptions:

1. **Second Sphinx:** the target is the Khentkaus-area natural bedrock parent mass, not simply the tomb map point. The tomb point remains only a proxy for exploratory mapping.
2. **Western alternative:** keep the western-plateau mass as a separate unknown candidate; do not merge it with Khentkaus.
3. **Half-face geometry:** retain all eight half-face patches on every pyramid. The H1/H2 ideals deliberately supply missing measurements as explicit parameters.
4. **Sagitta formula:** for total fitted-direction difference \(\Delta\), use \(c=(B/2)\tan(\Delta/2)\), not the earlier quarter-angle form.
5. **Map coordinates:** mixed-source map pins do not establish sub-metre relations. They are retained as approximate search anchors only; the ideal parent-mass distances are constrained by the exact design invariant.
6. **Quarry circle:** the real quarry is approximate and irregular; the exact circle is a mathematical ideal for registration, not a claim that the ancient cut boundary is a perfect circle.

## 15. Current conclusion

The most coherent purpose layer now visible is a **large-scale geometrical operating system for the plateau**: the natural outcrops supply geological and topographic reference masses; the Khentkaus-centred quarry and the Khufu side registrations organise extraction; the survey holes and trenches establish external datums and successive corrections; the three pyramid bases and eight half-faces belong to a single registered geometry.

This explains why the site contains not only enormous finished structures, but also carefully selected natural masses, quarry cuts, external reference features, causeway ramps, alignments and correlated stratigraphic surfaces.

The larger purpose of the entire system remains the next problem. Continue from this integrated architecture into the full functional network; do not collapse the whole system into “tombs” or into quarry logistics alone.

## 17. The Sphinx grid is a recoverable geometric dataset

The ARCE Sphinx Project established a local Sphinx grid:
- origin at \(N3000,E500\), defined 3.5m in front of the Sphinx south forepaw;
- grid orientation based on magnetic north observed on 20 February 1978;
- 63 points were triangulated to plot the head;
- profiles through the statue were recorded at 5m intervals;
- bedrock contours were drawn at 25cm intervals, and the surrounding back/top contours at 10cm;
- geological maps cover Members I, II and III, including a dip-plane map of Member II through the Sphinx amphitheatre;
- the vertical datum was tied to survey pins at the Khufu pyramid corners: adding 9.331m to the local Sphinx elevation gives height above mean sea level.

This means the Sphinx is not limited to the current map proxy. The archive includes precisely the data needed to reconstruct the present bedrock core and then infer a family of parent-outcrop envelopes.

### Required horizontal transformation

Do not assume the Sphinx grid is already identical to GPMP. Its origin is local and its initial orientation is magnetic north. Compute a two-dimensional similarity/rigid transformation using common survey control points:

\[
\begin{pmatrix}E_{\rm GPMP}\\N_{\rm GPMP}\end{pmatrix}
=
\begin{pmatrix}t_E\\t_N\end{pmatrix}
+
s
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\
\sin\theta&\cos\theta
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}u_{\rm Sphinx}\\v_{\rm Sphinx}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

Estimate \(t_E,t_N,\theta,s\) from at least two common horizontal control points (preferably more, with a least-squares residual), preserve the magnetic-to-true orientation correction, and verify the fit against independent holdout points. The vertical transformation is already tied to the Khufu corner pins, but must retain the source datum and offset.

Once tied, replace the Sphinx map proxy with multiple geometric datums: head centre, head base, neck strata, body bedrock envelope, quarry enclosure boundary and selected parent-mass reference points.

## 18. Reproduction update

The updated driver now:
- reads all ten GLSS B markers as paired binary/ternary integers;
- reconstructs their centroid, convex hull, plan dimensions and fitted surface plane;
- computes direct B-centroid distances to all three pyramid centres;
- recalculates the Khufu–Sphinx / Khafre–Khentkaus cross-pair relation with the GLSS datum;
- projects Sphinx and Khentkaus onto the Khufu–Menkaure axis;
- retains the Sphinx map proxy separately until the ARCE-to-GPMP transformation is solved.
