# 05 — Cross-domain relation register: Giza, Rishat and open-scale RSE

## 1. Purpose

This section connects exact mathematics with natural and archaeological questions without turning numerical correspondences into physical conclusions.

Policy:
- compare relations, not raw magnitudes alone;
- use dimensionless groups when quantities have different dimensions;
- state the physical mechanism required for each proposed coupling;
- preserve competing models;
- distinguish a shared physical law from a demonstrated connection between locations;
- search without a preset scale ceiling or floor, but do not assume every scale is connected.

## 2. Giza structural grouping: 3:2

The current research classification selects:
- three major pyramids: Khufu, Khafre, Menkaure;
- two additional system elements for comparison: the Great Sphinx and the Khentkaus monument/complex.

This creates a structural count of 3:2.

Important archaeological distinction: Khentkaus I is not classified archaeologically as a second sculpted sphinx. In this research grouping it is a second selected element to compare functionally with the Great Sphinx. Keep the user's functional grouping visible while preserving the archaeological classification.

The count ratio 3/2 is exact. It does not prove equal roles, a shared physical class, a designed ratio, morphological equivalence, or an electrical/acoustic/mechanical function.

To test the grouping, compare all six cross-links from the three pyramids to the two selected elements. For each link, record geometry, materials, path, time, functional hypothesis, measured response and uncertainty.

## 3. Rishat–Giza hypothesis

The current project hypothesis distinguishes:
- Rishat as a candidate naturally formed inorganic organizer of geological/electromagnetic properties;
- Giza as a candidate natural-plus-architectural configuration that may exploit or transform some of the same environmental affordances;
- the intervening environment as a possible part of a wider system, rather than a geometric gap explained only by distance.

What is established:
- both locations are subject to the same laws of electromagnetism;
- their geology and materials differ;
- Rishat has documented igneous/concentric geological structures;
- Giza has carbonate bedrock, limestone and granite components and an archaeological/architectural setting.

What is not established:
- a direct electromagnetic connection between Rishat and Giza;
- a common engineered or natural signal path;
- that the separation was chosen to delay or route a physical flow;
- that one site is a source and the other a receiver;
- that a project parameter of 0.2939775 Hz is a measured carrier between them.

The same laws operating at two sites do not demonstrate a causal link between the sites.

## 5. The RSE multi-scale rule

The RSE network is not a fixed hierarchy of objects. It is an open set of participants, fields and processes. Candidate dependencies may cross astronomical and galactic dynamics, planetary and lithospheric processes, geophysics and hydrology, structures and materials, grains and pores, molecular interfaces and atomic states, biological organization and human systems.

No scale relationship is presumed solely from geometric resemblance. The connecting mechanism may be absent, may change with scale or may require an effective theory.

## 8. Required falsifiers

- If independent geometry fails to reproduce an alleged ratio within survey uncertainty, reject that geometric correspondence.
- If a measured response is not coherent or phase-related under a specified source and transfer mechanism, reject that coupling.
- If a relation disappears under harmless graph relabelling, it is not a graph invariant.
- If similar correspondences occur as frequently in randomized or comparison datasets, reduce the evidential weight.
- If the claimed effect requires more input power than the proposed source can supply, reject the physical model.
- If a link has no units, carrier/mechanism or observable output, retain it as an undefined hypothesis, not a physical result.

## 9. Core question

The goal is not to force Giza, Rishat, Earth, a galaxy or a microscopic system into one numerical template. The goal is to find whether independently defined relations form a consistent network, and where that network breaks.
