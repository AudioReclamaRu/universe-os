# Phase 29 — bounded trace-identity ideal closure

## Objective

Phase 29 moves from a catalog of universal FTI identities to a bounded algebraic consequence space. It implements three exact operations:

1. Substitution closure: substitute rational linear combinations of noncommutative matrix words into the d-dimensional fundamental trace identity.
2. Multiplicative closure: multiply every bounded seed identity by every declared trace monomial in a finite multiplier vocabulary.
3. Additive closure: compute the exact rational linear span of all generated trace polynomials and return an independent basis with certificates for dependent candidates.

The outcome is an exact basis of a finite bounded vector space of universal trace relations. It is not called a minimal generating set of the full infinite T-ideal.

## Theoretical basis

In characteristic zero, the Procesi–Razmyslov theorem states that the relations among trace invariants of generic n × n matrices are generated, in the T-ideal sense, by the fundamental trace identity (equivalently, the polarized Cayley–Hamilton trace relation). See:

- Yu. P. Razmyslov, “Trace identities of full matrix algebras over a field of characteristic zero,” Mathematics of the USSR-Izvestiya 8:4 (1974), 727–760. [MathNet record and English PDF](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/im1989).
- C. Procesi, “The invariant theory of n × n matrices,” Advances in Mathematics 19:3 (1976), 306–381. DOI: [10.1016/0001-8708(76)90027-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/0001-8708(76)90027-X).
- A modern exposition of the characteristic-zero trace-invariant relation theorem is given in [Symmetric products, linear representations and trace identities](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13366-021-00577-0).

This theorem supplies the conceptual reason to close the FTI under substitutions and multiplication. Phase 29 implements only explicitly bounded slices of those operations.

## Exact rational linear-combination substitutions

Each FTI argument may be a finite rational linear combination of noncommutative words:

\[
P_i=\sum_{w\in S_i}c_{i,w}w,\qquad c_{i,w}\in\mathbb Q.
\]

The FTI is multilinear and symmetric in its d+1 arguments. Therefore:

\[
FTI(P_0,\ldots,P_d)=
\sum_{(w_0,\ldots,w_d)\in S_0\times\cdots\times S_d}
\left(\prod_i c_{i,w_i}\right)FTI(w_0,\ldots,w_d).
\]

Tuples with repeated words are retained. Argument permutations produce the same FTI, so every word multiset is represented in Phase 28's complete multiset catalog for the declared vocabulary. This is an exact span statement; the program does not try to enumerate infinitely many rational coefficient vectors.

The standalone expansion function verifies the resulting primitive integer polynomial with Phase 27's generic-matrix-entry verifier.

## Bounded multiplicative closure

The multiplier vocabulary consists of canonical cyclic trace words over the declared generators up to a maximum word length. Multiplier monomials are multisets of those trace words whose total matrix-letter degree is at most the configured bound.

For each verified Phase 28 seed identity R and each multiplier monomial m, Phase 29 constructs mR. Every derived relation is then independently passed to Phase 27's exact generic-entry verifier.

This creates a finite family of ideal consequences. It does not enumerate unbounded trace multipliers.

## Exact additive closure and basis certificates

Trace polynomials are represented sparsely over canonical products of cyclic trace words, with exact rational coefficients. An incremental sparse Gaussian-elimination procedure over Q retains independent candidate relations.

When a candidate is dependent, the package returns a coefficient vector over previously accepted candidates. It recomputes the complete sparse trace-polynomial sum and checks that every coordinate cancels exactly to zero.

The independent basis is thus a basis of the generated candidate span within the chosen bounds. It is not claimed to be a minimal T-ideal generating set; the latter requires an ideal/module-level minimality analysis beyond finite vector-space row reduction.

## Explicit bounds and unresolved semantics

Separate limits apply to:
- Phase 28 word catalog, substitution sets and FTI permutations;
- maximum word length in FTI substitutions;
- maximum multiplier trace-word length and total multiplier matrix-letter degree;
- number of multiplier monomials and derived relations;
- per-relation and total exact generic-entry expansion work.

A cap produces an unresolved or partial status. It is never interpreted as a negative mathematical result. Previously verified relations remain certified.

The Phase 27 expansion counter measures generic index assignments. It is a deterministic work proxy, not wall-clock time or coefficient-bit complexity.

## Scope

A completed result is exact for the finite family generated by:
- all FTI substitutions over the declared finite word vocabulary;
- all trace-monomial multipliers within the declared degree bounds;
- rational linear combinations of those generated relations.

This phase does not compute the full infinite ideal, does not claim a minimal T-ideal generating set, and does not yet feed these relations into Phase 24–26 adaptive graph refinement. It makes no claim that a physical structure implements the abstract matrix algebra.

## Tests

Fourteen standard-library tests cover rational linear substitutions, repeated arguments, zero supports, substitution caps, bounded multiplier closure, exact basis production, explicit linear-dependency certificates, cyclic trace-word canonicalisation, multiplier and seed caps, total expansion budget handling and input validation.
